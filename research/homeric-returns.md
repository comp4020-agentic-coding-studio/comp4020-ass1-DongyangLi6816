# The other captains' returns — fact sheet

Research note, not part of the deployed site. Facts only; no interpretation,
theme, or design decisions are recorded here.

Companion to `odyssey-journey-facts.md`, which covers Odysseus's own wanderings.
This sheet covers what the *Odyssey* says about **everyone else's** voyage home
from Troy — the material in Books 3 and 4 that the journey sheet marks as out of
scope.

## Sources consulted

- Homer, *Odyssey*, trans. Samuel Butler, prose — Books 1–5, 7, 10, 13, 16, 19,
  21, 23, 24: [classics.mit.edu/Homer/odyssey.html](http://classics.mit.edu/Homer/odyssey.html)
- A. T. Murray, Loeb Classical Library translation, consulted where Butler's
  rendering is loose. Every divergence is noted at the line concerned.

Where Butler and the Greek disagree, the Greek reading is recorded and Butler's
divergence is flagged. Butler is loose in at least three places that matter to
this sheet, all marked below.

## Coverage statement

This sheet enumerates **13 return-items (N1–N13)** — every Greek captain or
contingent whose return from Troy the *Odyssey* describes or mentions. It is a
full enumeration of the poem, not a selection. A further **6 items (E1–E6)** are
listed and excluded, each with the reason.

**Durations are stated for only 3 of the 13.** The other ten are gaps and are
left empty rather than estimated.

This is Homer's subset, **not the complete *nostoi* tradition**. See "What the
poem does not contain" below.

## Listed and excluded

- **E1 — Ajax son of Telamon**: died *at* Troy, not on the return; a shade in
  Hades (Bk 11). No nostos exists.
- **E2 — Achilles, Patroclus, Antilochus**: "Our best men all of them fell
  there" (Bk 3). Died at Troy; no return.
- **E3 — Amphilochus**: appears only in a genealogy of Melampus (Bk 15). No
  return narrated.
- **E4 — Eumelus**: named only as Iphthime's husband, "and lived in Pherae"
  (Bk 4). Already home; no voyage.
- **E5 — Philomeleides**: a Lesbian wrestler Odysseus threw (Bk 4). Not a
  captain, not a nostos.
- **E6 — Teucer, Calchas, Leonteus, Polypoetes, Podalirius, Menestheus**:
  **entirely absent from the *Odyssey***, verified by name-count across all 24
  books (zero hits each). They belong to the lost *Nostoi* and Apollodorus.

## The three stated durations, with quotes

### N2 — Diomedes, the fourth day

Butler, Book 3:

> "Four days later Diomed and his men stationed their ships in Argos, but I held
> on for Pylos, and the wind never fell light from the day when heaven first
> made it fair for me."

⚠️ **Butler is loose here.** The Greek (*τετράτῳ ἤματι*) and Murray's Loeb read
*on the fourth day*: "It was the fourth day when in Argos the company of
Diomedes, son of Tydeus, tamer of horses, stayed their shapely ships." "Four
days later" (an interval) and "on the fourth day" (an arrival date) are
different claims. **Use the Greek reading and note Butler's divergence.**

⚠️ It is **unresolved whether day four counts from Geraestus or from the start
of the Euboea crossing**. The text does not settle it. Leave it open rather than
picking one.

### N3 — Menelaus, in the eighth year

Butler, Book 4:

> "for it was nearly eight years before I could get home with my fleet. I went
> to Cyprus, Phoenicia and the Egyptians; I went also to the Ethiopians, the
> Sidonians, and the Erembians, and to Libya where the lambs have horns as soon
> as they are born, and the sheep lamb down three times a year."

⚠️ Butler's "nearly eight years" is loose; the Greek is **"in the eighth
year"** (Murray: "I brought my wealth home in my ships and came in the eighth
year").

⚠️ **Menelaus was not seven years.** Book 3 corroborates the eighth year
independently, and the same passage is the source of a common confusion — the
"seven years" there belongs to **Aegisthus's reign**, not to any voyage:

> "For seven years after he had killed Agamemnon he ruled in Mycene, and the
> people were obedient under him, but in the eighth year Orestes came back from
> Athens to be his bane… and on that very day Menelaus came home, with as much
> treasure as his ships could carry."

Also stated in Book 4: Menelaus was **becalmed twenty days** at Pharos.

### N5 — Odysseus, the twentieth year

Stated five times: Bk 2 (Halitherses' prophecy), Bk 16, Bk 19 (twice), Bk 21,
Bk 23, Bk 24. Representative, Bk 19:

> "Twenty years are come and gone since he left my home, and went elsewhither."

The war and the departure, Bk 5 (Hermes to Calypso):

> "He says that you have here the most ill-starred of alf those who fought
> **nine years** before the city of King Priam and sailed home **in the tenth
> year** after having sacked it."

⚠️ **"Ten years of wandering" is INFERRED, never stated.** Computation:
20 (total, stated) − 10 (Troy: nine years' fighting plus the sack in the tenth,
stated) = **10, INFERRED**. The one place Butler's text says "ten years'
absence" (Bk 16) sits inside a *simile* about a father and son — it is not about
Odysseus, and must not be cited for this.

Component durations stated elsewhere: seven years with Calypso, leaving in the
eighth (Bk 7); a full twelvemonth with Circe (Bk 10); the suitors in the house
three years (Bk 13).

## The poem's own summary of the returns

Proteus to Menelaus, Book 4:

> "only two of the chief men among the Achaeans perished during their return
> home. As for what happened on the field of battle- you were there yourself. A
> third Achaean leader is still at sea, alive, but hindered from returning."

Homer asserts: **2 chief men died on the return** (Ajax, Agamemnon), **1 still
at sea** (Odysseus), **the rest got home**. Odysseus is explicitly the outlier,
not a representative case.

## Flagged uncertainties

1. **N6 "Ajax the Lesser" is INFERRED.** The poem never calls him "the Lesser"
   or "son of Oileus." "Oileus" appears nowhere in Butler's text, and Murray at
   4.492ff gives no patronymic. The identification rests on two grounds: Ajax son
   of Telamon is already dead in Hades (Bk 11), and the Athena-wrath plus Gyrae
   details match the Locrian Ajax in the wider tradition. **Label inferred
   wherever used.**
2. **N7 "Neoptolemus" is Butler's supplement.** The Greek says only "the famous
   son of great-hearted Achilles" (~3.188–189). Book 4 independently corroborates
   that he got home: Menelaus is marrying his daughter to Achilles' son, who is
   "reigning" over the Myrmidons.
3. **Agamemnon's watchman "had been looking out for a whole year"** — that is
   **Aegisthus's vigil, not Agamemnon's voyage.** Do not convert it into a
   voyage duration.
4. **Homer's fair-wind passages are heroic, not historical.** Telemachus's
   overnight Ithaca→Pylos run (Bk 2 into Bk 3) covers a measured 197.5 km,
   implying **7.6–10.7 knots, INFERRED** — above even the single-ship figures in
   `voyage-baseline.md`, let alone the fleet band. Book 4 also places Pharos a
   day's sail off Egypt when it sits on the Alexandrian shore. Both the geography
   and the speeds break under measurement.

## What the poem does not contain

A disconfirming search ("complete list of nostoi", "which Greek heroes had
difficult returns from Troy") surfaced six figures with well-attested returns
that the *Odyssey* never mentions: **Teucer** (exiled from Salamis, settled
Cyprus), **Menestheus** (king of Melos), and the overland group **Calchas,
Leonteus, Polypoetes, Podalirius, Amphilochus**, who left their ships at Ilium
and walked to Colophon, where Calchas died after losing a prophecy contest to
Mopsus. All six were checked against the poem by name-count across all 24 books
and are absent. They come from the lost epic *Nostoi* (Proclus's summary) and
Apollodorus, *Epitome* 6.

**Anything built on this sheet is showing Homer's subset and must say so.**

The same search surfaced two claims that **contradict** Homer and must not be
used: that Neoptolemus travelled overland with Andromache, and that Philoctetes
was expelled from Meliboea. Neither is in the *Odyssey*, which says both simply
got home safely.
