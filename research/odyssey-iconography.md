# Which episodes ancient art depicted — fact sheet

Research note, not part of the deployed site. Facts only; no interpretation,
theme, or design decisions are recorded here.

Records which stops of Odysseus's voyage survive in ancient visual art, how well
attested each is, and whether a usable free image exists. Gathered to decide
image sourcing for the prototype.

## Sources consulted

- **LIMC** (*Lexicon Iconographicum Mythologiae Classicae*) article index — 2,451
  headings; catalogue-number counts harvested via the LIMC Foundation's
  photographic archive (`iconiclimc.ch`), which exposes catalogue numbers in its
  keyword strings.
- Wikimedia Commons categories and file licence pages.
- Susan Woodford on recognisability devices in Greek art.
- Steven Lowenstam on the relation of epic narrative to its images.
- Rafn (LIMC "Kalypso" entry) on the Calypso corpus.

⚠️ The **Beazley Archive Pottery Database was gated** and could not be queried.
All counts below are **lower bounds** from LIMC only.

## Coverage statement

All **14 stops** assessed, none skipped. Result: **5 well-attested, 6 rare,
3 none found.**

## A hypothesis that was tested and FAILED

The working hypothesis was:

> "The ancients painted the monsters and the violence, but nobody painted the
> waiting — the long stays (Calypso's seven years, Circe's year) are
> under-represented or absent, while the brief violent episodes are heavily
> depicted."

**The evidence contradicts it.** Recorded here because the retraction matters
more than the guess.

The comparison that matters, both Odyssey-specific and neither inflated:
**Kirke ≥71 against Polyphemos I ≥66.** The year-long stay outnumbers the
Cyclops.

Further against the hypothesis: the Ithaca cluster (Penelope + suitors +
Eurykleia + Telemachos + Eumaios + Laertes + Philoitios) totals ≈119 entries, of
which **only the mnesterophonia is violent** — foot-washing, the loom, Eumaios
and Laertes are all domestic.

Calypso's ≥17 span Athens NM 354 (Melian/Parian krater, c. 615 BC), Berlin F
2379 (Attic), Volos MB 15/16 (Hellenistic Homeric relief bowls), an Etruscan
grave stele from Felsina (Bologna, c. 500–450 BC), a Praenestine bronze mirror,
two Etruscan sarcophagi (Perugia 403, 404), two Pompeian wall paintings (VII
4.21-22; VII 9.7-8, the Macellum), a Pantikapaion tomb painting, and four gems.

⚠️ Some are contested: LIMC gems Kalypso 15/16/18 are **co-catalogued as
Penelope 34/35a/35b** — the same objects indexed under both women, which is what
an unresolved seated-woman identification looks like in the data.

## What survives of the hypothesis, and is better supported

Rafn's observation on the Calypso corpus stands: surviving images "concentrate
almost exclusively on Odysseus's departure from her," and **no certain ancient
image of a seated Calypso survives**. Calypso was depicted perhaps seventeen
times and essentially always at the moment of leaving.

Lowenstam gives the mechanism: **"art has only one tense."** Episodes without a
pregnant instant lose out regardless of content.

That is a claim about **duration having no iconography**, not about subject
matter — and unlike the original hypothesis it is backed by a positive corpus
rather than an absence.

## Two further explanations, both better than the hypothesis

**Recognisability (Woodford).** Devices that make a scene legible: inscriptions,
attributes, a strangely formed adversary, normal elements abnormally combined,
context within a cycle. Her worked Odyssey example — Odysseus under the ram: "to
see a man riding upside-down beneath a sheep… makes it clear that something
quite out of the ordinary is going on… He might be recognised by his traveller's
hat (*pilos*), which became something of a trademark, but his situation is
actually very much more revealing."

This also explains the Laestrygonian anomaly: **rock-throwing giants have no
attribute distinguishing them from any gigantomachy.** Not "insufficiently
violent" — insufficiently legible.

**Survival and export bias.** Over 30,000 Greek pots come from Etruscan tombs,
and Etruscan buyers took cups and essentially no lekythoi, so production
proportions cannot be read off survivals. Concretely: **Odysseus/Uthuze, the
Etruscan Odysseus, has ≥148 catalogue numbers against Greek Odysseus's ≥191.** A
large slice of "Odyssey iconography" reflects Etruscan funerary demand, not
Greek painters' preferences.

## Hard negatives — index-absence, not merely search-absence

The LIMC article index (2,451 headings) has **no article at all** for:

- **Lotophagoi** (Lotus-Eaters)
- **the Cicones**
- **Charybdis**
- **Thrinakia / Cattle of Helios**
- **Ogygia** (Calypso is covered under "Kalypso" instead)

This is a stronger form of negative evidence than a failed search.

## Practical availability

**Ogygia remains unillustratable from antiquity in practice.** MANN 81839 has no
Commons file, and none of the seventeen LIMC objects surfaced with a free
photograph.

⚠️ **The ancient artwork is public domain by age; the modern photograph of it
may not be.** Licence must be checked per file. Institutional PD releases (the
British Museum) differ from CC-BY uploads (e.g. the Circe antidote image
credited to a Flickr CC-BY-2.0 source).

## Unverified — do not cite

The Buitron-Oliver 1992 table of contents used earlier as "effectively a
canonical enumeration" **could not be verified**; the Stanford record carries no
contents note. It was an unverified search summary. The LIMC article index does
